================================================================================
 STUDY REPORT
 Self-Supervised Representation Learning under Varying Data Distributions
 for Reduction of Artefact-Driven False Alarms in Rodent EEG Seizure Detection
================================================================================

Document status : LIVING DOCUMENT. This file is updated as each method,
                  parameter, and result is finalised during implementation.
                  Values marked [TBD] are fixed on the validation partition
                  before any test-set evaluation and recorded here when set.
Companion work  : Baseline project DL_WITH_SSL_GA (Multi-Scale TCN / M3).
                  This study reuses the M3 encoder, data pipeline, and
                  evaluation utilities of that project without modification,
                  so that the baseline (R0) is a valid reference point.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MOTIVATION AND PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Continuous single-channel rodent EEG monitoring produces recordings in which
annotated seizure activity occupies well under 1% of total time (raw
ictal:non-ictal ratio approximately 1:438 in the baseline corpus). To train
the baseline Multi-Scale TCN (M3) under this extreme imbalance, the baseline
project applied PROXIMITY-AWARE DOWNSAMPLING (baseline STUDY_REPORT Section 9;
create_balanced_splits.py): all ictal segments and all non-ictal segments
within +/-60 s of a seizure ("near-seizure") are retained, while non-ictal
segments further than 60 s from any seizure ("far-from-seizure") are randomly
subsampled to only 5% of the near-seizure count.

Consequence. The resulting training corpus (220,653 segments; ictal:non-ictal
1:2.37) is dominated by peri-ictal background (95.3% of the non-ictal class).
Behavioural artefacts -- grooming, chewing, movement, electrode/muscle
activity -- occur predominantly during normal behaviour spread across the
whole recording, i.e. mostly FAR from seizures. Because the far pool is
reduced to roughly 3.3% of the corpus, the vast majority of isolated artefact
windows are discarded. The classifier is therefore trained almost exclusively
to discriminate seizure from near-seizure background and seldom learns the
appearance of artefacts. At deployment on the full, un-downsampled stream,
transient high-morphology artefacts are effectively out-of-distribution and
are frequently mis-fired as seizures. This artefact-driven FALSE ALARM is the
failure mode targeted by the present study.

Central idea. Exposing the ENCODER to the full data distribution -- including
artefacts in their natural abundance -- through self-supervised representation
learning (SSL) should yield a representation in which artefacts are separable
from seizures, thereby reducing false alarms without requiring artefact
labels (which do not exist: artefacts are unlabeled members of the non-ictal
class).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. OBJECTIVES AND HYPOTHESES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Primary objective. Determine whether, and by what mechanism, self-supervised
pretraining on broader data distributions reduces artefact-driven false alarms
in rodent EEG seizure detection, relative to the supervised M3 baseline.

Hypotheses.
  H1  A self-supervised objective improves the representation even when the
      pretraining data is held identical to the baseline (R0 -> R1).
  H2  Exposing the encoder to the full distribution (with artefacts) improves
      artefact separation beyond the objective alone (R1 -> R2).
  H3  Allowing the classification head and decision threshold to also see a
      broadened background unlocks false-alarm reduction that a narrow
      fine-tune cannot (R2 -> R3).
  H4  Any gain is not merely an effect of broader fine-tuning data without SSL
      (R4 vs R0).
  H5  (Headline) With both models exposed to broad fine-tuning data, SSL
      pretraining reduces false alarms relative to no SSL (R3 vs R4).
  H6  SSL improves the learned REPRESENTATION, not only the optimisation /
      initialisation (frozen linear-probe vs full fine-tune, all runs).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DATA DISTRIBUTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All distributions derive from the same preprocessed segment store of the
baseline project: single-channel EEG, 500 Hz, 5-second windows (2,500 samples)
with 50% overlap, 50 Hz notch, DC removal, robust z-score normalisation. Labels
are binary (ictal / non-ictal); artefacts are unlabeled non-ictal segments.

  D0     Proximity-aware downsampled corpus (baseline training manifest,
         data_splits_nonictal_sampled.json). 220,653 segments; near-seizure-
         heavy; far-from-seizure background ~3.3%. This is the exact set on
         which R0 was trained.

  D_full Full unlabeled corpus (~33M segments; the untouched seizure/ and
         non_seizure/ directories in natural abundance). Contains artefacts at
         their true prevalence. Used ONLY for self-supervised pretraining
         (labels are not consumed by SSL).

  D_broad Broadened-background fine-tuning corpus. D0 with the far-from-seizure
         sampling fraction substantially enlarged (and/or an artefact-rich
         stratified supplement), so that the classification head and threshold
         are exposed to diverse background during supervised fine-tuning.
         Labeled. Construction parameters: [TBD, fixed on validation].

The held-out validation and test partitions are the FULL, un-downsampled
stream and are never downsampled, so evaluation reflects deployment conditions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. ENCODER ARCHITECTURE (REUSED FROM BASELINE, UNCHANGED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The encoder is the Multi-Scale TCN (M3) of the baseline project
(tcn_utils.py, class MultiScaleTCN), reused without architectural change so
that R0 remains a valid reference and all runs share an identical backbone.

  * Three parallel dilated causal-convolution branches (fine / intermediate /
    coarse), dilations [1,2,4] / [8,16,32] / [32,64,128]; three CausalConvBlocks
    per branch; LayerNorm over channels; GELU; spatial Dropout1d; residual
    connections. Coarse branch receptive field ~5.38 s spans the full window.
  * Fusion (concat + 1x1 conv, or average) -> global average pooling over time
    -> single linear classification head nn.Linear(num_filters, 1).
  * Encoder / head separation is built in via return_embedding=True, which
    returns the pooled (batch, num_filters) feature vector and skips the head.

Two tap points are used:
  (i)  pooled vector  (batch, num_filters)          -- classification, probing,
                                                       embedding analysis.
  (ii) pre-pool feature map (batch, num_filters, T) -- available for span-level
                                                       reconstruction if needed
                                                       by the SSL decoder.

Hyperparameters (num_filters, kernel_size, dropout, fusion) are inherited from
the baseline's selected configuration so that the encoder capacity is identical
across R0-R4.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. SELF-SUPERVISED REPRESENTATION LEARNING METHOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Method: Masked EEG Autoencoding (masked-span reconstruction)

The encoder is pretrained by masking contiguous spans of the 5-second input
window and reconstructing the original signal from the unmasked context. A
lightweight decoder is attached to the encoder for pretraining and discarded
afterwards; only the encoder weights are carried forward.

5.2 Rationale (why masked reconstruction rather than contrastive learning)

Contrastive representation learning (e.g. SimCLR/TS-TCC/TS2Vec) was considered
and rejected as the primary method for three task-specific reasons:

  (a) Mechanistic alignment. To reconstruct masked spans drawn from the full
      distribution, the encoder must internally model artefact morphology.
      This maps directly onto the study's premise -- "expose the encoder to
      the full distribution."
  (b) No pair construction. The corpus is ~99% background; contrastive
      negatives are almost all background, so instance discrimination pushes
      apart semantically identical background windows (the false-negative
      problem), injecting label noise. Masked reconstruction requires no
      positive/negative pairs and is immune to this.
  (c) No augmentation invariance. Contrastive learning teaches invariance to
      its augmentations; an augmentation that can turn seizure-like into
      artefact-like morphology would collapse the very distinction the study
      seeks to sharpen. Masked reconstruction imposes no such invariance.

Masked autoencoding is thus both more suitable and more defensible for the
false-alarm objective, while remaining a mainstream, well-cited SSL paradigm.

5.3 Pretraining configuration

  * Masking: contiguous span masking; masking ratio [TBD; candidate sweep
    25% / 50% / 75%, fixed on validation].
  * Reconstruction target: time-domain signal (primary). A spectral (STFT
    magnitude) reconstruction term is an optional extension, recorded here if
    adopted.
  * Loss: reconstruction error on masked positions (e.g. MSE), [exact form TBD].
  * Optimiser / schedule / batch size / epochs: [TBD]; checkpoint-resume
    mirrors the baseline's pattern for long HPC jobs.
  * Which runs pretrain on which data: R1 on D0; R2 and R3 on D_full.
  * Held constant across R1-R3: masking scheme, decoder, loss, optimiser,
    schedule. Only the ENCODER INITIALISATION and the DATA vary between runs.

5.4 Data provenance and no-leakage policy (pretraining)

  * SSL pretraining GRADIENTS come from TRAINING-PARTITION SUBJECTS ONLY.
    Although the objective is label-free, no gradient update ever uses
    validation or test signals, preserving the baseline's subject-level split
    and eliminating encoder-side leakage.
  * The VALIDATION PARTITION is used to monitor and select the SSL checkpoint
    via reconstruction loss (no gradients). Monitoring on validation is
    preferred over a held-out training slice because it measures reconstruction
    generalisation to UNSEEN SUBJECTS. Validation therefore serves as the
    development set throughout the study (SSL monitoring + fine-tune early
    stopping).
  * The TEST PARTITION is untouched during both pretraining and fine-tuning; it
    is the only fully held-out set and supplies all headline numbers.

5.5 Training diagnostics reported (pretraining)

  * Train and validation RECONSTRUCTION LOSS curves per epoch (masked-position
    error). These are the pretraining convergence/overfitting diagnostics.
  * Note: F1-score does not apply to pretraining (no labels); the pretraining
    objective is reconstruction loss.

5.6 Hyperparameter policy: FIXED, DISCLOSED, CONSTANT ACROSS RUNS

  Definition. "SSL knobs" are the hyperparameters that exist only because of
  the self-supervised pretraining stage and have no counterpart in the baseline
  supervised M3 (and are therefore not covered by M3's Optuna search):
    - masking ratio (fraction of the window masked);
    - mask span length (length of each contiguous masked segment);
    - decoder architecture (depth/width of the discarded reconstruction head);
    - reconstruction loss form (MSE vs L1; time-only vs +spectral term);
    - SSL optimiser schedule (pretraining LR, warmup, batch size, epochs);
    - fraction of D_full consumed (subsampling for the ~33M-segment job).

  Policy. This study does NOT tune the SSL knobs to maximise performance.
    (i)   The encoder architecture and the supervised fine-tuning recipe are
          inherited UNCHANGED from the selected M3 configuration, so encoder
          capacity is identical across R0-R4.
    (ii)  The SSL knobs are fixed to literature-informed defaults for masked
          autoencoding, DISCLOSED in this document, and held IDENTICAL across
          R1-R4. They are not searched on downstream validation F1.
    (iii) Because the SSL recipe is held constant, the only quantities that
          vary between R1, R2 and R3 are the ENCODER INITIALISATION and the
          DATA distribution -- preserving the one-variable-at-a-time logic of
          the run matrix (Section 9).

  Rationale. This is a controlled causal study of data-distribution effects,
  not a leaderboard optimisation. Two practices are therefore explicitly
  avoided because they would confound or weaken the causal claim:
    - Per-run tuning (different knobs for R1 vs R2 vs R3) would confound
      "data exposure" with "different recipe", destroying the R1->R2->R3
      contrast.
    - A large downstream-validation search would invite the criticism that
      gains stem from tuning rather than from SSL/data, and -- since R0/R4 use
      fixed recipes -- would introduce an asymmetric advantage.
  Fixing and disclosing the knobs is both the most defensible option and the
  scientifically stronger one, because the controlled comparison is most
  convincing when only the studied variable moves.

  Permitted exception (optional). A SINGLE, transparent masking-ratio sweep
  {25% / 50% / 75%} may be reported as an ABLATION (characterising sensitivity,
  not selecting a winner). If run, the chosen ratio is decided once and then
  held constant across all runs; the sweep is reported in full. This is the
  only place any SSL knob is varied, and it is framed as robustness
  characterisation, not tuning.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. DOWNSTREAM ADAPTATION PROTOCOLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

After pretraining, the SSL decoder is discarded and the linear classification
head is attached. Each SSL run is adapted under two protocols:

  P1  Frozen (linear probe). Encoder weights are frozen; only the linear head
      is trained. Isolates the quality of the learned REPRESENTATION.

  P2  Full fine-tune. Encoder and head are trained end-to-end. Matches the
      baseline deployment regime and measures the initialisation benefit.

The supervised fine-tuning recipe (loss BCEWithLogitsLoss, optimiser, LR
schedule, early stopping, post-processing) is inherited unchanged from the
baseline M3 training, so that the only differences from R0 are the encoder
initialisation and, in R3/R4, the fine-tuning data distribution.

6.1 Data usage and no-leakage policy (fine-tuning)

  * Fine-tuning gradients come ONLY from training data (D0 or D_broad, built
    from training-partition subjects).
  * The validation partition is used ONLY for early stopping and model
    selection -- never for gradient updates.
  * NO thresholding is performed. The decision rule is fixed at P > 0.5, as in
    the baseline project; the threshold is not tuned on validation.
  * No post-processing hyperparameter is selected on validation either; event
    metrics are instead reported across a range of min-event durations
    (Section 8.1). The test partition is evaluated once, at the end.

6.2 Training diagnostics reported (fine-tuning; every trained run)

  * Train and validation LOSS (BCEWithLogitsLoss) curves per epoch.
  * Validation MACRO-F1 per epoch -- the early-stopping / model-selection
    metric (as in the baseline). F1 is a selection/monitoring metric, NOT the
    differentiable training objective (which is BCE); this distinction is kept
    explicit in the write-up.

8. EVALUATION FRAMEWORK (THREE LAYERS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All evaluation is performed on the held-out validation/test partitions of the
FULL, un-downsampled stream. Layer 1 uses the baseline eval_utils.py unchanged
for direct comparability with M3.

8.1 Layer 1 -- Standard performance metrics (eval_utils.py)

  Segment level : accuracy, precision, recall (sensitivity), specificity,
                  Youden's J, macro-F1, AUROC, AUPRC, segment false-alarm rate.
  Event level   : per-mouse chronological predictions are post-processed by a
                  FIXED, ORDERED four-stage pipeline, then matched to ground
                  truth by any-overlap -> precision, recall/sensitivity, F1,
                  false alarms per hour (far_per_hour_event), mean detection
                  latency.

                  Post-processing order (applied in this exact sequence):
                    1. Probability smoothing        (moving window = 3 segments)
                    2. Minimum-event filtering       (drop events < min-event; see
                                                      MIN-EVENT SWEEP below)
                    3. Refractory-period merging     (merge events < 30 s apart)
                    4. Maximum-duration filtering    (drop events > 120 s)
                  The decision rule P > 0.5 is applied to the smoothed
                  probabilities (after stage 1) to form candidate events.

                  MIN-EVENT SWEEP. Event metrics are reported at min-event
                  durations of 10, 15, 20, 25, and 30 s (as in the baseline
                  project), rather than fixing a single value. The baseline
                  selected 25 s because it reduced false positives but at the
                  cost of missing short-duration seizures; the present study
                  aims for stronger performance at SHORTER min-event durations,
                  so the full sweep is reported to make the sensitivity / false-
                  alarm / latency trade-off explicit across durations. This
                  sweep is a REPORTING choice, not a validation-based selection.

8.2 Layer 2 -- Artefact-proxy stratified false-alarm number

Because artefacts are unlabeled, a label-free proxy identifies non-ictal
segments that are almost certainly artefacts AND morphologically resemble
seizures (the segments most likely to trigger false alarms).

  Stratum A ("seizure-mimicking non-ictal"). Non-ictal (label 0) segments that
  are BOTH:
    * far from any seizure: min distance to nearest seizure > 60 s (candidate
      margin 120 s), using the baseline min_distance_to_seizure() logic; AND
    * morphologically seizure-like, per a MULTI-FEATURE morphology score rather
      than a single amplitude threshold. Candidate features (single-channel-
      valid): line length (high-frequency / muscle activity), kurtosis (spiky /
      electrode-pop transients), signal envelope amplitude (movement), variance
      / power, and a high-band power ratio (EMG band). Features are standardised
      across the non-ictal pool; segments whose morphology lies within the
      seizure feature manifold (near the seizure feature-centroid / high
      composite score) are selected.

  A single high-amplitude or high-variance threshold is deliberately NOT used,
  as it misses muscle/electrode artefacts that are not the largest-amplitude
  events.

  Stratum B (control). Far-from-seizure, LOW-morphology quiet background. If
  false alarms fall on A but not on B, the improvement is specific to hard
  artefacts rather than a global threshold shift.

  Metric. For each trained model: segment-level false-alarm rate on A, and,
  after the standard post-processing, false-alarm events per artefact-hour on
  A; reported alongside B.

  Guardrails.
    * The stratum is defined ONLY from signal statistics and seizure distance,
      never from any model's predictions (no circularity).
    * All thresholds/percentiles are fixed on validation before test.
    * A random sample (~50-100 segments) of stratum A is visually audited to
      confirm the segments are genuine non-seizure artefacts; the audit is
      documented here.

8.3 Layer 3 -- Representation (embedding) analysis

Purpose: show WHY false alarms fall -- that the SSL encoder separates
artefacts from seizures in feature space where the baseline overlaps them.

  * Extract frozen-encoder pooled embeddings (num_filters-dim) for a balanced
    held-out sample across groups: seizure, artefact-proxy (A), quiet
    background (B), and optionally peri-ictal near-seizure.
  * Encoders compared: ALL runs -- R0 (baseline M3, loaded from DL_WITH_SSL_GA),
    R1, R2, R3, and R4. Including R1 is essential: because R1 applies the SSL
    objective on the artefact-poor D0 while R2 applies it on the full
    distribution, the R0 -> R1 -> R2 progression visually distinguishes whether
    artefact/seizure separation arises from the OBJECTIVE (H1) or from
    full-distribution DATA EXPOSURE (H2). R3/R4 complete the broad-fine-tune
    contrast.
  * Encoder state analysed: for SSL runs (R1-R3) the frozen encoder as used
    under the linear-probe protocol (the representation as learned by SSL); for
    R0/R4, which have no SSL stage, their trained supervised encoder. This
    asymmetry is unavoidable (R0/R4 have no encoder to freeze) and is stated
    explicitly in the write-up.
  * Qualitative: UMAP (primary) and t-SNE (secondary) 2-D projections coloured
    by group.
  * Quantitative (computed in the FULL embedding space, not the 2-D
    projection, to avoid reliance on a single figure):
      - seizure-vs-artefact linear-probe AUROC (logistic probe on frozen
        embeddings): higher => better separation;
      - silhouette score and/or Fisher ratio between the seizure and artefact
        clusters.
